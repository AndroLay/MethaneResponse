# Earth rendering: terrain, atmosphere, and mobile performance

## What the controls do

The existing MapLibre map remains the only map renderer. The Earth Rendering panel adds:

- **Auto, Eco, Balanced, and Quality** profiles. Auto uses available browser hints (`navigator.deviceMemory`, CPU cores, viewport width, and device-pixel ratio); these are estimates, not GPU benchmarks. Pixel ratio is capped at 1, 1.5, or 2 for low, medium, or high profiles. The map tile cache is also reduced on low-power profiles.
- **Mapterhorn terrain**, added only when the current zoom is close enough. The raster DEM uses Terrarium decoding. Auto enables terrain at zoom 6.2 for high and 7.5 for medium; manual On uses zoom 5.8 on the globe and 4.7 on a flat projection. Low-power mode disables terrain. Exaggeration is a visual setting, not an elevation measurement.
- **MapLibre sky and atmosphere** while Globe projection is selected, with Natural and Cinematic looks. The satellite raster receives a restrained saturation/contrast adjustment in Natural and a stronger display treatment in Cinematic; neither changes scientific records. Atmosphere blend changes with zoom. MapLibre documents sky styling as experimental and under development, so browser and GPU output can differ.
- **UTC solar shading and stable directional light.** Hillshade uses a fixed viewport-relative direction so camera movement does not move the apparent light. Night-side shading is off by default. When enabled, the geometry and approximate day/night boundary are recalculated every five minutes; overlay opacity is capped by render tier (0.12 low, 0.16 medium, 0.20 high). Use **Center on UTC day/night line** to bring the terminator into view; **Reset view** returns to the MARS data overview. This is a visualization of solar position, not nighttime satellite imagery, a physical radiative simulation, or an emissions observation. MapLibre's global light affects compatible extruded layers, not the satellite raster itself.
- **Subtle space backdrop** around the globe. Star points are decorative UI only; they do not represent astronomical observations.
- **Optional, date-pinned NASA GIBS context tiles:** MODIS Terra true color, MODIS Terra cloud fraction, and AIRS methane at 400 hPa. These layers are off by default and paused in low-power mode. The default date is seven days before the current UTC date because daily products can arrive with a delay; a date with no published tiles can render blank, in which case choose an earlier date. The initial overlay opacity is 38% and can be changed with the control. Outside low-power mode, a compact date and interpretation badge remains on the map while the settings panel is closed. AIRS at 400 hPa is upper-atmosphere context; it does not locate or measure a surface leak and is not a UNEP MARS plume observation.
- **Tilt 3D** and **Reset view** camera controls. If DEM initialization or tiles fail, the map returns to a flat surface and keeps its source markers available.

Low-power mode pauses full source-density and flux heat surfaces and NASA context tiles while preserving the basemap and a compact flux-cell view when selected. Night-side shading remains off unless the user enables it. Manual Quality can increase rendering cost; it does not promise a particular frame rate.

## Methane data overlays and freshness

- **Source location density** colors where MARS source records are located. It counts source records; it is not methane concentration or plume extent.
- **Reported flux overlay** uses individual MARS plume rows with valid coordinates and positive numeric `kg/h` values. Medium/high tiers draw a flux-weighted historical observation-density surface; overlapping records blend, so its colors do not mean a continuous local flux value. Low-power mode instead summarizes reported values as median flux at the centroids of approximately 1° grid cells. Cell circles are symbols, not measured plume boundaries. Zero and missing flux do not contribute heat; individual plume records still retain their exact numeric, zero, or missing values and uncertainties.
- **Historical playback** exposes a monthly timeline while viewing reported flux or individual plume records. It cumulatively reveals archive rows through each month; its small bars count records, not emissions. Months with no rows remain explicit gaps, not zero-emission periods. Playback is historical animation and never a live feed.
- **Investigation priority** loads the hash-checked `/api/intelligence/rankings` snapshot and colors matched sources across the full pinned MARS release. The score is an uncalibrated heuristic for investigation ordering, not a leak likelihood. Selected-source analysis also shows a separate conservative anomaly screen and its abstention state. A distinct historical model estimates linked MARS archive-record recurrence in the Evidence Auditor; the map does not present that estimate as a physical-emissions forecast. No validated future physical-emissions forecast is available.
- The map's **30 days** and **1 year** observation filters are anchored to the latest day in the loaded archive, not the computer's current date. This keeps a historical snapshot useful after its retrieval date while avoiding any suggestion that older data is live or continuous.
- The map marks the live feed **OFF** and shows the latest observation date in the loaded dataset. The MARS snapshot is historical; it is not a streaming alert service. Existing NASA MODIS cloud fraction remains date-pinned context imagery and is labeled with its selected date; it is not live weather. AIRS methane at 400 hPa remains upper-atmosphere context only.

## Data services and attribution

- Terrain is fetched on demand from [Mapterhorn's public TileJSON endpoint](https://tiles.mapterhorn.com/tilejson.json); map attribution identifies Mapterhorn. See [Mapterhorn attribution](https://mapterhorn.com/attribution/) and [data access](https://mapterhorn.com/data-access/). Availability, quotas, and service-level guarantees have not been established for this prototype.
- Optional context tiles come from [NASA GIBS](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/access-basics/). The chosen date and layer are included in the tile URL, and each source has an attribution string. Tile availability depends on the selected product/date, NASA service availability, and network access.
- During a browser check on 2026-10-12, AIRS tiles for 2026-10-08 returned HTTP 404 while the same layer's visible tiles for 2026-10-05 returned HTTP 200. NASA GIBS can return missing-date tiles without a MapLibre `error` event, so the panel tells users to try an earlier date when an overlay is blank. This point-in-time check is not a publication-lag guarantee.
- In a separate browser check on 2026-10-12, MODIS Terra cloud-fraction tile requests for selected dates 2026-10-05, 2026-09-30, and 2026-09-20 returned HTTP 400. The cause was not isolated, so this does not establish that those dates or the GIBS service are generally unavailable. Keep cloud context optional and date-pinned; treat an error/blank layer as unavailable context and continue to show the historical MARS observations. NASA documents that dated tile requests depend on the selected layer, date, matrix set, and tile coordinates; direct service availability still needs a fresh check in the target network.
- Existing MARS source and plume records continue to come from the pinned, attributed casebook described in [`data/real/README.md`](../../data/real/README.md). These map context layers do not add case evidence, prove mitigation, or change the casebook's provenance or license.

## Reproducible checks and limitations

Run from the repository root:

```sh
bun test apps/web/src/earth-rendering.test.ts
bun run check
```

The pure helper tests cover device-profile caps, terrain zoom thresholds and Terrarium encoding, UTC solar position and polygon bounds, dated NASA tile URLs, date validation, and opacity limits. They do not benchmark a GPU or prove that remote tile services are reachable.

Earlier browser smoke on 2026-10-12 used sandboxed Chromium with the available GPU. It observed Mapterhorn DEM and NASA GIBS AIRS tiles returning HTTP 200 at selected visible tiles and checked a 390×844 viewport. That evidence predates the current Earth look and adaptive-rendering edits below; it is not evidence that the current version has been visually rechecked.

Before making mobile-performance claims, inspect Globe and flat modes on physical Android and iOS devices, including a low-memory phone. Check tilt, DEM activation after close zoom, projection and basemap changes, optional-tile outages, context loss, and thermal/battery behavior. No 60-FPS target or physical-phone result is claimed by this implementation.

- The dashboard opens in Globe mode where WebGL is available. Software WebGL (for example, Chromium SwiftShader) can render the globe using the low-power tier; an actual context or shader failure still switches to the flat map. No-WebGL environments retain the case and evidence panels.
- Natural satellite tiles use a slight saturation and contrast lift; Cinematic applies a stronger display treatment. Display adjustments affect imagery only and never change MARS measurements.
- Esri World Imagery is a geographic-context mosaic; capture date and resolution vary by area. The UI says it is not methane evidence and does not claim one acquisition date for the whole globe.
- Records with missing numeric flux use a neutral gray marker and a separate legend entry; they are not styled as zero-flux observations.
- Terrain exaggeration is moderate. Optional hillshade is limited to close zoom on the Quality tier. Auto quality samples frames only while the map moves; after 24 intervals it adjusts a tier when at least 35% exceed 34 ms or at most 10% exceed that threshold, with a 30-second cooldown. This heuristic is not a physical-device benchmark.

## Globe accuracy and renderer choice

MapLibre's globe projection renders a unit sphere. It preserves the source longitude/latitude data and is appropriate here for geographic context, but it is not a WGS84 ellipsoid geodesy engine. CesiumJS uses a WGS84 ellipsoid by default and exposes terrain, imagery and atmosphere on its globe. A move to Cesium would be justified if this product adds ellipsoid-based measurement, camera-height analysis, or high-resolution 3D terrain as core requirements. This pass keeps MapLibre because the current application depends on its existing cluster, plume, source-selection, and raster-overlay layers; changing engines would require rebuilding and revalidating those integrations.

MapLibre globe reference: [projection implementation notes](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/API/type-aliases/ProjectionData/). Cesium reference: [Globe API](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Globe.html) and [WGS84 overview](https://cesium.com/platform/).
